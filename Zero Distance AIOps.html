01 · The problem this replaces

Every incident starts the same way: a person, alone, grepping.

Across our storefronts, an error can originate in checkout, surface in a tax-compliance integration, and get diagnosed by whoever happens to be on call — reading raw Java stack traces across five separate log shares, by hand, under pressure.

⏱

Time-to-understanding is the bottleneck

Not time-to-fix. Most incident time is spent finding which log, on which server, said what — before anyone can even start fixing anything.

🧠

The fix lives in one person's head

The engineer who solved this exact error six months ago is the fastest path to resolution today — if they're reachable, and if they remember.

📉

Leadership sees tickets, not impact

"500 error in DefaultErrorHandler.java:57" means nothing to the business. What failed for the customer, and how many customers, is buried underneath.

02 · What's already built and running

Eight working screens, one automated pipeline.

This isn't a prototype or a slide-ware concept — it's a running FastAPI + Angular platform with a Postgres-backed data model spanning 18 entities, already monitoring live production log traffic end to end.

Live

Real-time log ingestion

Watches local and network-share log directories continuously; handles rotation, partial writes, and multiline stack traces automatically.

Live

AI root cause analysis

Every ERROR/FATAL log is auto-analyzed: category, severity, root cause, business impact, and a fix — immediate, short-term, and permanent.

Live

Auto-triage engine

Weighs business impact, technical impact, recurrence frequency, and AI confidence into one risk score and a P1–P4 priority — no human gatekeeping needed.

Live

Incident deduplication

Fingerprints every failure signature; a recurrence reuses the known fix instead of re-spending an AI call on a problem we've already solved.

Live

Knowledge base (RAG)

Every resolved incident is embedded and indexed, so tomorrow's RCA is informed by every incident that came before it.

Live

GitLab auto-filing

Priority incidents become GitLab issues automatically — titled, labeled, and pre-filled with the full analysis. No copy-paste triage.

Live

Teams & email alerts

The right people are notified the moment something is decided to matter, with AI-drafted summaries instead of raw log dumps.

Live

Data-safe by design

Customer, order, account and site identifiers are masked before any text reaches an external AI vendor — shipped this cycle.

03 · How it works

One path from a raw log line to a filed, prioritized ticket.

The platform makes exactly one cost-sensitive decision per failure: has this exact problem been seen before? If yes, the fix is reused for free. If no, it earns a fresh AI analysis — and becomes reusable knowledge for next time.

04 · Strategic fit

This is the mechanism for Zero Distance to Customer.

"Zero Distance" means nothing sits between a customer's problem and the organization's awareness of it. Today that distance is measured in escalation emails and tribal knowledge. Every capability below shortens it directly.

  • Business-language impact, not stack traces — every analysis includes a business_impact field written for a non-engineer, e.g. "customers intermittently could not complete checkout," so leadership sees customer effect, not exception names.
  • Detection to diagnosis in seconds, not hours — the moment an error is written to a log file, it's ingested, scored, and (if it matters) root-caused before a human is even paged.
  • The complaint is traceable, precisely — real-world example already surfaced in production: a checkout failure traced through blPricingWorkflow to an India tax-compliance API timeout, for a specific order, in one pass.
  • Nothing repeats silently — recurring failures are automatically grouped, so "this has happened 12 times this month" is a fact the business has, not a guess.

"An error occurred during the blPricingWorkflow workflow… Fulfillment Price Exception: Failed to get the token for calling the India tax compliance API — connection timed out."

Actual production error, auto-classified P2 and auto-triaged — the kind of failure that used to surface as a vague customer complaint days later.

07 · Customer support & trust

Faster answers for support, without exposing customer data to a vendor.

Support and SRE both need to trace "what happened to this customer's order" — but that data should never be a line item in a third-party AI vendor's logs. Both are true here simultaneously.

Support gets answers fast

A support agent investigating an order can be pointed to the exact log entry, service, and — if it triggered an incident — the full root cause and fix status, instead of filing a ticket into engineering and waiting.

The AI never sees the customer

Shipped this cycle: customer, order, account, site and user identifiers, plus email addresses, are automatically redacted from every prompt and embedding sent to Claude, Gemini, or any future provider — while remaining fully intact and searchable in our own systems.

(site:27379) (customer:1569260120) (order:30060948) → (site:***MASKED***) (customer:***MASKED***) (order:***MASKED***) — masked automatically, in real time, before the request leaves the network. Internal storage and Support tooling are unaffected.

This is a genuine differentiator against buying an off-the-shelf AIOps tool: we control exactly what a third party ever sees.
08 · Roadmap

From "explains what happened" to "prevents it happening again."

The platform's own engineering notes already scope the next architectural step (event-driven notifications). The rest of this roadmap is the natural extension of the pipeline that already exists.

Now01

Event-driven notification bus

Replace the current synchronous call chain (triage → GitLab → Teams → Email) with a publish/subscribe model, already designed internally, so a slow or failing channel never blocks another. Adds Slack as a first-class channel.

Effort: S–MReliability
Now02

Customer-impact rollup dashboard

Aggregate open incidents by estimated customer/order reach and revenue-sensitive workflow (checkout, tax, fulfillment) — the single view a CEO or CTO would actually open.

Effort: MExecutive visibility
Next03

Predictive / anomaly-based alerting

Move from "an ERROR was logged" to "this service's error rate is trending toward an incident" — catching degradation before it becomes a customer-visible failure.

Effort: LPrevention
Next04

ChatOps: ask the platform directly

"Why did checkout fail for order #X" answered inline in Slack/Teams by the RAG knowledge base, without opening the dashboard — support and on-call get answers where they already work.

Effort: MSupport enablement
Next05

Guarded auto-remediation

For known, low-risk, high-confidence incident types (e.g. a documented pool-exhaustion restart), let the platform propose — and, behind an approval gate, execute — the fix it already knows.

Effort: LMTTR → near-zero
Later06

Multi-tenant / multi-brand rollout

Generalize beyond this estate so any business unit can onboard its own log sources, GitLab project, and notification routing without a code change.

Effort: LPlatformization
Later07

Fine-tuned, fully on-prem model

Train a smaller model on our own resolved-incident history to replace external AI calls entirely for routine cases — near-zero marginal cost and full data residency by default, not by exception.

Effort: LCost + compliance
09 · Risks & how they're handled

Where this can go wrong, and what already stops it.

RiskMitigation already in place / planned
AI gets the root cause wrongEvery analysis carries a confidence score; low-confidence or ambiguous cases route to REQUIRES_HUMAN_REVIEW rather than auto-closing. Nothing auto-remediates today — it recommends.
Sensitive data reaches an AI vendorCustomer/order/account/site/email data is masked in-flight before any external call, on by default, with a kill switch for edge cases.
Vendor price hikes or an outageProvider-agnostic by design — Claude, Gemini, and a fully local Ollama model are already interchangeable with a settings change, not a redeploy.
Alert fatigue from over-notifyingThe triage engine gates GitLab/Teams/Email behind priority thresholds (P2+), not every ERROR line — noise is filtered before it reaches a human.
Single points of failure in the pipelineAddressed directly by the Now-phase move to an event-driven bus, so one slow downstream channel can't stall the others.
10 · The ask

Fund this as a product, not a side project.

The hard part — real-time ingestion at scale, multi-provider AI orchestration, deduplication, auto-triage, and a working UI — is already built and already watching production. What's needed now is the investment to harden and extend it deliberately, rather than it continuing to grow ad hoc.

Dedicated ownership

A named product owner and 1–2 engineers to execute the "Now" and "Next" roadmap phases on a committed timeline, instead of best-effort.

Provisioned AI budget

A predictable monthly ceiling for Claude/Gemini usage at full production scale, sized against the cost comparison above — still an order of magnitude below a commercial suite.

An executive sponsor

Air cover to make this the default incident path org-wide, not an opt-in tool one team happens to use.
